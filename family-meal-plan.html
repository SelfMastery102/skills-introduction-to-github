🌾 Our Family Table

A one-year nutrition & meal-prep plan for Mom, Dad & Baby — rooted in Manitoba, Canada

🥩 Grass-fed & pasture-raised 🌱 Clean, whole-food sources 🍁 Manitoba seasonal & local 👶 Baby-solids journey: 5 → 16 months

How this plan works

A simple, repeatable rhythm: one big prep on Sunday, fresh dinners that double as next-day lunches, and baby foods made from the same clean ingredients the parents eat — no separate "baby aisle" needed. The plan runs June 2026 → May 2027, following Manitoba's seasons and your baby's growth from 5 months to about 16 months.

🥩

Clean proteins first

  • Grass-fed & grass-finished Manitoba beef and bison
  • Pasture-raised chicken, turkey & eggs
  • Wild-caught Manitoba pickerel (walleye), lake trout & whitefish
  • Pasture-raised pork in moderation
🥕

Whole, natural foods

  • Seasonal Manitoba vegetables & prairie fruit (saskatoons, raspberries, sour cherries)
  • Root-cellar staples for winter: squash, carrots, beets, potatoes, cabbage
  • Manitoba oats, wild rice, hemp hearts, lentils
  • Butter, olive oil, full-fat dairy if tolerated — nothing ultra-processed
👶

One kitchen, three eaters

  • Baby eats simplified versions of family meals (no added salt, sugar or honey before 12 months)
  • Iron-rich first foods — key for breastfed babies at 6 months
  • Purées → mashes → soft finger foods → family table

The everyday plate

½ vegetables & fruit — seasonal, local where possible
¼ protein — grass-fed meat, wild fish, eggs, legumes
¼ whole grains & starchy veg — oats, wild rice, potatoes, squash

Plus healthy fats (butter, olive oil, avocado) and water as the main drink. Vitamin D matters on the prairies — especially October to April.

👩

Mom

  • ~2,100–2,500 kcal/day (more if breastfeeding: +350–500 kcal)
  • Priorities: iron, omega-3 (fish 2×/week), calcium, vitamin D, plenty of fluids
  • Snacks built into the plan — no running on empty
👨

Dad

  • ~2,400–2,800 kcal/day depending on activity
  • Protein at every meal; bigger portions of the same dishes
  • Hearty prairie staples: bison chili, roasts, pickerel fry
👶

Baby (5 months now)

  • Breast milk or formula stays the main food all year
  • Solids begin around 6 months, per Health Canada guidance
  • By 12 months: 3 small meals + snacks from the family table

Baby's solids journey — 5 to 16 months

Baby is 5 months old now. Health Canada recommends starting solids at about 6 months, when baby shows readiness signs — so this first month is for watching and preparing, not feeding.

Safety essentials: no honey before 12 months (botulism risk) · no added salt or sugar · no whole nuts, hard raw veg or other choking hazards · introduce common allergens (peanut, egg, fish, dairy, wheat) one at a time, early and often, ideally starting around 6 months · always feed seated and supervised · this page is a planning aid, not medical advice — confirm details with your doctor or public-health nurse.
5 months · June

Watch & prepare

Milk only. Look for readiness: sits with little support, good head control, reaches for food, lost the tongue-thrust reflex.

  • Buy: small blender or food mill, silicone ice-cube trays, soft spoons, bibs, suction bowl
  • Let baby join the table at mealtimes to watch you eat
6 months · July

First tastes — iron first

Start with iron-rich foods once a day, 1–2 teaspoons, working up.

  • Puréed grass-fed beef or bison (simmered, blended with broth)
  • Iron-fortified oat cereal made with Manitoba oats
  • Single veg purées: carrot, squash, zucchini from the garden/market
  • Begin allergen introductions: well-cooked egg yolk-and-white purée, smooth peanut butter thinned into purée
7–8 months · Aug–Sep

Thicker mashes & harvest flavours

  • 2 meals/day → mashed, lumpier textures
  • Flaked Manitoba pickerel (bones carefully removed), lentil mash, mashed saskatoons & raspberries
  • Soft finger foods: steamed carrot sticks, ripe pear slices, toast strips
  • Offer water in an open cup at meals
9–11 months · Oct–Dec

Finger-food explorer

  • 3 meals/day plus milk feeds; baby largely self-feeds soft pieces
  • Shredded pot-roast beef, meatballs, scrambled egg, soft-cooked squash cubes, beans
  • Mini versions of family stews and soups (set baby's portion aside before salting)
12 months · January

Joining the family table 🎉

  • Eats the family meals, chopped small; whole pasteurized milk can be offered
  • Honey now okay; still keep salt and sugar low
  • 3 meals + 2 snacks; milk feeds taper naturally
13–16 months · Feb–May

Confident toddler eater

  • Same food as parents — just cut safely (halve grapes/cherries, no whole nuts)
  • Practice with a spoon and fork; expect mess, keep offering variety
  • Appetite swings are normal — parents decide what & when, toddler decides how much

The weekly prep routine

About 2½ hours on Sunday sets up the whole week. Tick items off as you go — progress saves on this device.

🛒 Saturday — market & shop (45 min)

Farmers' market in summer, freezer + root cellar + grocery in winter.

🍳 Sunday — big prep (2–2½ h)

Oven, stovetop and slow cooker all running at once.

👶 Sunday — baby food block (30 min)

From ~6 months on. One cube tray ≈ a week of one food.

🌙 Weeknight rhythm (15–25 min/night)

Because the heavy lifting is already done.

Tip: cook dinner once, eat twice — every dinner in the year plan is portioned so leftovers become the next day's lunch.

Sourcing clean food in Manitoba

Where to find grass-fed, pasture-raised and wild foods around the province. Buying direct from producers is usually cheaper per pound and you know exactly how the animal was raised.

🐂 Grass-fed beef & bison

Direct from farm · freezer quarters/halves
  • Search "grass-finished beef Manitoba" — many farms in the Interlake, Parkland and southwest sell by the quarter or half (budget a chest freezer)
  • Manitoba is bison country: lean, iron-rich, ideal for chili, burgers and baby's first meat purée
  • Ask: 100% grass-fed and finished? No routine antibiotics/hormones? Dry-aged?

🐔 Pastured poultry & eggs

Farm gate · farmers' markets
  • Pasture-raised whole chickens freeze well — buy a season's worth in fall
  • Farm-gate eggs from hens on pasture: deeper yolks, great for baby's allergen introduction
  • Local turkey for holidays and ground-turkey weeknights

🐟 Wild Manitoba fish

Lake Winnipeg & northern lakes · Freshwater Fish Market, fishmongers
  • Pickerel (walleye) — the prairie classic, mild enough for baby
  • Lake trout & whitefish — excellent omega-3 sources
  • Aim for fish twice a week for mom and dad; flaked, deboned portions for baby from ~7 months

🥬 Vegetables, fruit & pantry

St. Norbert & community farmers' markets · CSA shares · U-pick
  • June–September: CSA veggie box + market hauls; U-pick saskatoons, strawberries, raspberries, sour cherries — freeze flat on trays
  • October: stock the cold room — squash, carrots, beets, cabbage, potatoes, onions
  • Manitoba-grown staples year-round: oats, hemp hearts, lentils, wild rice, honey (12 m+), sunflower oil

💰 Budget notes

  • A half beef or bison (~90 kg) typically costs far less per pound than retail grass-fed cuts and covers most of the year's red meat
  • Whole chickens beat parts: roast once, then soup + sandwiches + bone broth
  • Frozen summer berries and fall root-cellar veg keep winter produce costs (and food miles) down
  • Bone broth from every roast = free soup base and a mineral-rich liquid for baby purées
