Family guide — baby's solids journey & Manitoba sourcing

Every recipe card includes a 👶 Little Sous-Chef note so baby eats from the same pot. Here's the bigger picture.

Safety essentials: Health Canada recommends starting solids around 6 months · no honey before 12 months · no added salt or sugar in baby's portion · no whole nuts or hard round foods · introduce allergens (egg, peanut, fish, dairy, wheat) one at a time, early and often · always seated and supervised · this is planning help, not medical advice — confirm with your doctor or public-health nurse.

Sourcing clean food in Manitoba

Buying direct from producers usually beats retail prices for grass-fed — and you know exactly how it was raised.

🐂 Grass-fed beef & bison

  • Farms across the Interlake, Parkland & southwest sell quarters/halves — budget a chest freezer
  • Bison: lean, iron-rich, perfect for chili, burgers and baby's first meat purée
  • Ask: 100% grass-fed and finished? No routine antibiotics or hormones?

🐔 Pastured poultry & eggs

  • Buy a season of pasture-raised whole chickens in fall; they freeze beautifully
  • Farm-gate eggs: deep yolks, ideal for baby's allergen introduction

🐟 Wild Manitoba fish

  • Pickerel (walleye), lake trout & whitefish from Lake Winnipeg and northern lakes
  • Fish twice a week for parents; flaked deboned portions for baby from ~7 months

🥬 Veg, fruit & pantry

  • June–Sept: farmers' markets, CSA box, U-pick saskatoons/strawberries/sour cherries — freeze flat
  • October: stock the cold room — squash, carrots, beets, cabbage, potatoes, onions
  • Manitoba staples year-round: oats, hemp hearts, lentils, wild rice, honey (12 m+)
